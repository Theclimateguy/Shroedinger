# Prior art for the FORM of a cross-scale envelope-coupling metric ("inheritance fidelity") and its use as a model-evaluation diagnostic

Metric under assessment (for reference): adjacent octave bands (200-400, 400-800, 800-1600 km) of 850 hPa relative vorticity from a Gaussian band-pass; |band| smoothed at the band's coarse edge, then log; spatial Spearman correlation between adjacent-band log-envelopes; median over time; anchored by subtracting the median over 99 Fourier-phase-randomised surrogates; forecast value compared with ERA5 at the same valid times. An internal audit found: rho +0.14 with the Mathis AM coefficient, rho -0.42 with a K-O cascade-correlation prediction, rho +0.69 with local intermittency (log-envelope variance).

Verification legend: [V] = bibliographic details checked in this session against a publisher, DOI resolver, PubMed or arXiv record. [V-partial] = existence and venue checked, some fields (pages or volume) taken from memory. [U] = not checked in this session, from memory; verify before citing.

## Q1. Amplitude modulation (AM) in wall turbulence: what it measures, and whether it has been used to validate LES, wall models or ML surrogates

### Takeaway
The Mathis-Hutchins-Marusic AM coefficient R correlates the large-scale *signal* u_L (signed) with the large-scale-filtered Hilbert envelope of the small-scale signal. It is not an envelope-envelope correlation. Its interpretation is contested: it is tied to skewness, it is a triadic-phase measure, and per Cui & Jacobi (2025) it is dominated by linear large-scale/mean-flow interaction rather than convective nonlinearity. AM appears in LES work in three ways: (a) LES datasets are *validated* against DNS/experiments and AM is then *quantified* in them; (b) AM is used as an *ingredient* of predictive wall models fed by LES; (c) LES is used to *study* AM. I found no study that uses R as a formal pass/fail skill metric for a wall model or an ML surrogate.

### Cited Findings
- Mathis, Hutchins & Marusic (2009), JFM 628:311-337, doi:10.1017/S0022112009006946 [V]. The method: decompose u at λx = δ into u_L and u_S; take the Hilbert envelope of u_S; low-pass filter the envelope at the same cutoff to get E_L(u_S); define R = <u_L E_L(u_S)> / (σ σ). R ≈ 0.33 at z+ = 15. The effect grows with Re (Re_τ ~10^3 to 10^6, the latter from atmospheric surface-layer data) — [Mathis et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022112009006946)
- Mathis et al. (2009) note that the Hilbert transform "has often been used in the study of geophysical and atmospheric processes" (Hristov et al. 1998; Huang et al. 1999). They also note that Sreenivasan (1985) used the envelope to characterise fine-scale intermittency — [Mathis et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022112009006946)
- Hutchins & Marusic (2007), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 365:647-664 [V-partial: citation as given in Mathis 2009; DOI 10.1098/rsta.2006.1942 is U]. This paper first observed AM-like behaviour — [cited in Mathis et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022112009006946)
- Schlatter & Örlü (2010), Phys. Fluids 22:051704, doi:10.1063/1.3432488 [V]. They show with experimental and synthetic signals that R is related to the skewness of the original signal, so it "may not be an independent tool to unambiguously detect or quantify" AM — [Schlatter & Örlü 2010](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/22/5/051704/257529/Quantifying-the-interaction-between-large-and)
- Mathis, Marusic, Hutchins & Sreenivasan (2011), Phys. Fluids 23:121702, doi:10.1063/1.3671738 [V-partial; article number U]. R is analogous to a cross-term in the scale-decomposed skewness — [cited in Doğan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1088/1873-7005/aaca81)
- Bernardini & Pirozzoli (2011), Phys. Fluids 23:061701, doi:10.1063/1.3589345 [V-partial; article number U]. They give a "refined measure": two-point covariance maps C_AM from DNS — [cited in Doğan et al. 2018 / arXiv 1812.03683](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.03683)
- Ganapathisubramani, Hutchins, Monty, Chung & Marusic (2012), "Amplitude and frequency modulation in wall turbulence", JFM 712:61-91, doi:10.1017/jfm.2012.398 [V] — [cited in Doğan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1088/1873-7005/aaca81)
- Duvvuri & McKeon (2015), "Triadic scale interactions in a turbulent boundary layer", JFM 767:R4, doi:10.1017/jfm.2015.79 [V]. They give a formal relation between skewness and R. Both measure phase in triadically consistent interactions — [Duvvuri & McKeon 2015](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2015.79)
- Cui & Jacobi (2025), JFM, doi:10.1017/jfm.2024.1203 [V]. Main claim: R "suppresses the signature of convective scale interactions" because of cosine weighting of the biphase. R is "mostly influenced by linear interactions between LSMs and the mean" and is "a metric for local deviations from Taylor's hypothesis". They propose R_T (derivative of u_L), weighted by sin(biphase) — [Cui & Jacobi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2024.1203)
- Doğan, Örlü, Gatti, Vinuesa & Schlatter (2018/2019), Fluid Dyn. Res. 51:011408, doi:10.1088/1873-7005/aaca81 [V]. A review of AM quantification. It compares Fourier filters with EMD on one well-resolved LES dataset (Re_θ 880-8200) and finds that the choice of filter changes R — [Doğan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1088/1873-7005/aaca81)
- Doğan et al. (arXiv:1812.03683): to test for false positives they randomly scramble the signal along the filter directions, which preserves the moments but destroys scale information. C_AM then drops, leaving only a small spurious inner peak for the Fourier filter. This is a *surrogate-null* idea close to the phase-randomised anchoring used in the inheritance metric — [arXiv 1812.03683](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.03683)
- Eitel-Amor, Örlü & Schlatter (2014), Int. J. Heat Fluid Flow 47:57-69, doi:10.1016/j.ijheatfluidflow.2014.02.006 [V]. A well-resolved LES of a turbulent boundary layer up to Re_θ = 8300, validated against DNS and hot-wire data. AM between inner and outer layers is *quantified* in the LES: the off-diagonal covariance peak has a stronger Re dependence than the diagonal one — [Eitel-Amor et al. 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0142727X14000319)
- Inoue, Mathis, Marusic & Pullin (2012), Phys. Fluids 24:075102, doi:10.1063/1.4731299 [V]. Wall-modelled LES log-region signals drive the Marusic-Mathis-Hutchins (2010, Science 329:193, doi:10.1126/science.1188765) predictive inner-outer model, which contains superposition and AM terms. The predicted near-wall spectra and moments up to 4th order are compared with experiments. Here AM is a *model ingredient*, not an evaluation metric — [Inoue et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4731299)
- Bose & Park (2018), Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. (WMLES review) [V-partial; doi:10.1146/annurev-fluid-122316-045241 is U]. They state that the AM-based model reconstructs inner-layer statistics from WMLES, "compared favorably with experimental measurements (Inoue et al. 2012)" — [Bose & Park 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6800697/)
- Ren, Laima & Li (2019), AIP Advances 9:105112 [V-partial]. They use WMLES of the atmospheric boundary layer at very high Re to compute the single-point R profile and compare its zero-crossings with prior experiments. This uses LES to *study* AM, with only implicit validation — [Ren et al. 2019](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/adv/article/9/10/105112/151728/Numerical-study-of-amplitude-modulation-in-the)
- Wu, Christensen, Fischer & Pantano (APS DFD 2019 abstract): AM framework used as a WMLES *boundary condition* for rough walls [abstract only] — [APS DFD 2019](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2019/a16/2/)

### Inferences
- In form, R ≠ the inheritance metric. R correlates a signed large-scale signal with a small-scale envelope at the same point (or two heights). The inheritance metric correlates two *log-envelopes* of adjacent bands across *horizontal space* and is anchored by surrogates. This fits the audit's weak rho = +0.14.
- The AM literature has two lessons directly transferable to the inheritance metric. (1) Envelope-type cross-scale correlations are entangled with odd/even higher moments: skewness for R; intermittency/log-envelope variance for the inheritance metric (audit rho +0.69). (2) Filter choice and false-positive nulls matter. The Doğan et al. randomisation test parallels the phase-randomised anchor.
- Cui & Jacobi (2025) is a strong caution against calling any envelope correlation a "scale interaction" or "inheritance" mechanism without a dynamical argument.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that uses R (or C_AM) as a *quantitative acceptance metric* comparing WMLES/LES or ML turbulence surrogates against DNS. A targeted search (WMLES, rough-wall, LES + AM) returned only uses as model ingredient or object of study. Absence is not proven; a citation-forward search on Mathis 2009 filtered for "LES" or "neural network" would be the next step.
- I did not retrieve Hutchins & Marusic (2007) directly. DOI marked U.

## Q2. Cross-frequency amplitude-amplitude coupling (AAC) in neuroscience; surrogate methods

### Takeaway
Bruns et al. (2000) introduced amplitude envelope correlation (AEC), which explicitly works "even among different frequencies". This is the closest named precedent for *envelope-envelope correlation between bands*, but it is in the time domain, between electrodes, for neural signals. The CFC literature has a mature surrogate-significance culture (Tort 2010; Aru 2015). It also has a specific warning (Hyafil 2015): negative AAC between *neighbouring* bands can be spurious, caused by frequency modulation. That warning applies directly to adjacent-octave envelope correlation.

### Cited Findings
- Bruns, Eckhorn, Jokeit & Ebner (2000), "Amplitude envelope correlation detects coupling among incoherent brain signals", NeuroReport 11(7):1509-1514, PMID 10841367 [V]. AEC "can detect signal coupling without phase coherence, even among different frequencies". It is applied to subdural recordings and reveals γ-coupling missed by coherence. (Note: the DOI suffix differs between sources: -00029 per the Exa/Crossref record vs -00028 per Europe PMC; check before citing.) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10841367/); [LWW](https://journals.lww.com/neuroreport/abstract/2000/05150/amplitude_envelope_correlation_detects_coupling.29.aspx)
- Bruns & Eckhorn (2004) studied envelope-to-envelope and envelope-to-signal correlations across bands (gamma-delta) in subdural recordings [described in Jirsa & Müller 2013; primary not retrieved, U] — [Jirsa & Müller 2013, Front. Comput. Neurosci.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2013.00078/full)
- Jensen & Colgin (2007) taxonomy of CFC types: power-power, phase-phase, phase-frequency, phase-power [as cited in Jirsa & Müller 2013; primary U] — [Jirsa & Müller 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2013.00078/full)
- Canolty & Knight (2010), "The functional role of cross-frequency coupling", Trends Cogn. Sci. 14(11):506-515, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2010.09.001 [V-partial: from reference list of Yeh et al. 2016] — [Yeh, Lo & Hu 2016 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4834901/)
- Tort, Komorowski, Eichenbaum & Kopell (2010), "Measuring phase-amplitude coupling between neuronal oscillations of different frequencies", J. Neurophysiol. 104, doi:10.1152/jn.00106.2010 [V; pages 1195-1210 are U]. A Kullback-Leibler modulation index. It reviews seven other CFC measures and discusses "the utility of surrogate control analyses" — [J Neurophysiol](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/jn.00106.2010); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2941206/)
- Aru et al., "Untangling cross-frequency coupling in neuroscience" (bioRxiv 2014, doi:10.1101/005926 [V]; journal version Curr. Opin. Neurobiol. 31:51-61, 2015 [U]). A clear spectral peak is a prerequisite for meaningful phase/amplitude interpretation. Statistical evaluation uses "suitable surrogate data". PAC can reflect non-stationarity — [Aru et al. bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/005926)
- Hyafil (2015), "Misidentifications of specific forms of cross-frequency coupling: three warnings", Front. Neurosci. 9:370, doi:10.3389/fnins.2015.00370 [V]. Warning 3: "frequency modulation of a signal can create spurious negative AAC between subcomponents of the signal extracted in neighboring frequency bands"; "any negative AAC implicated neighboring frequency bandwidths should be interpreted with extra caution" — [Hyafil 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00370)
- Yeh, Lo & Hu (2016), "Spurious cross-frequency amplitude-amplitude coupling in nonstationary, nonlinear signals" (Physica A, per PMC record; volume U) — [PMC4834901](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4834901/)
- Frontiers MEG PAC study (2016): surrogate choice changes PAC estimates. Block-swap surrogates (Aru et al.) are recommended as more conservative; PAC has even been "detected in atmospheric noise signals" — [Front. Comput. Neurosci. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2016.00120/full)

### Inferences
- In form, AEC across frequencies (Bruns 2000) is the closest neuroscience analogue: band-pass → envelope → correlation between bands. The inheritance metric differs in four ways: (i) spatial rather than temporal correlation; (ii) log-envelopes and Spearman rank; (iii) envelope smoothing at the coarse band edge, so both envelopes live on comparable scales; (iv) anchoring by subtraction of a phase-randomised surrogate median (an "excess over Gaussian" statistic) rather than a significance threshold.
- Hyafil's warning 3 maps onto the meteorological case. If the dominant scale of vorticity features shifts locally across the 400/800 km octave boundary (a local "frequency/wavenumber modulation"), adjacent-band log-envelopes become *anti*-correlated, without any cross-scale dynamics. Phase-randomisation destroys spatial localisation of such shifts, so the effect would survive anchoring. This is a candidate confound the author should test, for example with a synthetic field of single-scale packets whose wavenumber drifts in space.
- The CFC literature never uses AAC as a *model-evaluation* score for simulations against observations. It is a discovery and biomarker tool. Exception: Jirsa & Müller (2013) compare CFC in "real and virtual brain networks", i.e. model vs data, but for PAC, not AAC [title-level only].

### Gaps
- Not verified: Bruns & Eckhorn (2004) exact citation; the Aru et al. (2015) journal version; and whether any computational-neuroscience model-fitting study uses AAC (rather than PAC) as a fitting target.

## Q3. Multifractal cascades and intermittency in the atmosphere; were cascade statistics used to evaluate GCM/NWP/reanalysis realism?

### Takeaway
Yes. The Lovejoy-Schertzer group explicitly evaluated reanalyses (ERA-40, ERA-Interim, 20CR) and operational NWP models (CMC GEM, NOAA GFS) with multiplicative-cascade statistics: trace moments, C1, α, effective outer scale. In Stolle et al. (2009, 2012) this includes dependence on forecast lead time (t = 0, 48, 144 h). This is the most direct prior art for "a cross-scale (cascade) statistic used to compare forecasts with analyses". Their statistics are one-field moment-scaling (K(q)) exponents of a flux proxy, not correlations between band envelopes. The wavelet "space-scale correlation functions" of Arneodo et al. (1998) are the cascade-theory object closest in form to log-envelope cross-scale correlation.

### Cited Findings
- Stolle, Lovejoy & Schertzer (2009), "The stochastic multiplicative cascade structure of deterministic numerical models of the atmosphere", Nonlin. Processes Geophys. 16:607-621, doi:10.5194/npg-16-607-2009 [V]. Over almost all scales, and to within about ±1%, ERA40, GEM and GFS fields (u, T, h at 5 levels) follow multiplicative-cascade predictions. There is a "Levy collapse" within ±2-5%. Model differences are quantified: e.g. GEM α is about 0.20 smaller than ERA40 for humidity, and at 1000 mb α is ~1.65 for GEM vs 1.94 for ERA40. They note classical turbulence-theory tests on GCM/reanalysis data "fared rather poorly" — [preprint PDF](https://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~gang/eprints/eprintLovejoy/neweprint/npg-2009-14-version4.pdf); [DOI via Stolle 2012 refs](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1916)
- Lovejoy & Schertzer (2010), "Towards a new synthesis for atmospheric dynamics: Space-time cascades", Atmos. Res. 96(1):1-52, doi:10.1016/j.atmosres.2010.01.004 [V]. Table 1b intercompares 1000 mb GEM fields at integrations t = 0, 48, 144 h (C1, α, L_eff) — an explicit lead-time comparison — [Atmos. Res. PDF](http://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~gang/eprints/eprintLovejoy/neweprint/Atmos.Res.8.3.10.finalsdarticle.pdf)
- Stolle, Lovejoy & Schertzer (2012), "The temporal cascade structure of reanalyses and global circulation models", QJRMS 138(668):1895-1913, doi:10.1002/qj.1916 [V]. Temporal cascades in ERA40, GFS and GEM, with outer scales of 5-20 days. They quantify "the (typically small) differences in the cascades from model to model, as functions of latitude, altitude and forecast time" and discuss implications for ensemble forecasting and stochastic parametrization — [Stolle et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1916)
- Lovejoy & Schertzer (2011), "Space-time cascades and the scaling of ECMWF reanalyses: Fluxes and fields", JGR 116:D14117, doi:10.1029/2011JD015654 [V-partial; article number U]. They also note that analogous scaling analyses "have not yet been carried out for climate models" (as of 2011) — [JGR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011jd015654); [PDF](https://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~gang/eprints/eprintLovejoy/neweprint/JGR.ECMWF.2011.2011JD015654-1.pdf)
- Lovejoy & Schertzer (2013), The Weather and Climate: Emergent Laws and Multifractal Cascades, Cambridge University Press, doi:10.1017/CBO9781139093811 [V: from the Stolle 2012 reference list] — [ref list](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1916)
- Precipitation products (TRMM, CPC gauges, ECMWF, 20CR) were intercompared by cascade parameters. C1 for rain is ≈0.3-0.5, vs ≈0.05-0.10 for state variables. The authors conclude that product-to-product differences exceed deviations from scaling. This is a cascade-based *intercomparison/evaluation* of reanalysis precipitation (Lovejoy, de Lima & Schertzer 2012, Adv. Water Resour.; authors and volume U) — [PDF](https://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~gang/eprints/eprintLovejoy/neweprint/ADWR.SL.DS.2012.pdf)
- Arneodo, Bacry, Manneville & Muzy (1998), "Analysis of random cascades using space-scale correlation functions", PRL 80:708-711, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.80.708 [V]. A wavelet-based two-point *space-scale* correlation formalism, "particularly well suited to study multiplicative random cascade processes for which the correlation functions take a very simple expression". Applied to turbulence data — [PRL](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.80.708)
- Arneodo, Bacry & Muzy (1998), "Random cascades on wavelet dyadic trees", J. Math. Phys. 39(8):4142, doi:10.1063/1.532489 [V]. Correlation functions of wavelet coefficients across the dyadic tree characterise the underlying cascade — [JMP](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.532489)
- Arneodo, Manneville, Muzy & Roux (1999), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 357:2415-2438 [V]. Space-scale correlation functions applied to turbulent velocity; lognormal cascade cannot be distinguished from other models at accessible Re — [Phil Trans A](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsta/article/357/1760/2415/52593/Revealing-a-lognormal-cascading-process-in)

### Inferences
- **The closest *usage* precedent** for the inheritance metric's Phase-33 application is Stolle et al. (2009, 2012) and Lovejoy & Schertzer (2010, Table 1b). Cascade statistics computed on operational forecasts at several lead times are compared with reanalysis. The novelty claim must therefore be narrowed: "cross-scale statistics as a forecast-evaluation diagnostic" is *not* new. What may be new is (a) the specific envelope-coupling form, (b) the surrogate anchoring that isolates the non-Gaussian coupling from the spectrum, and (c) application to ML systems and ensemble means.
- **The closest *form* precedent** from cascade theory is Arneodo et al. (1998). There, correlations between log|wavelet coefficients| at different scales and positions are predicted analytically for multiplicative cascades (logarithmic decay in scale separation). The audit's "K-O cascade-correlation prediction" (rho -0.42) is presumably a variant of this; the Arneodo papers are the canonical citation for that null model.
- The audit's link to intermittency (rho +0.69) fits cascade theory. In a multiplicative cascade, the covariance of log-amplitudes across scales is governed by the same intermittency coefficient (C1 or λ²) that sets the log-amplitude variance.

### Gaps
- I did not find a Lovejoy-group or other study applying cascade statistics to *ML* weather models (GraphCast/Pangu etc.) or to *ensemble-mean* forecasts. A search for "intermittency/multifractal AI weather models" returned spectral-only evaluations. This may be an open niche, but the search was not exhaustive.
- Not verified: Lovejoy et al. (2012) precipitation-paper bibliographic details; Lovejoy & Schertzer (2011) article number.

## Q4. Wavelet-based cross-scale diagnostics and envelope methods in meteorology (Buschow-Friederichs, bicoherence, Hilbert envelopes)

### Takeaway
Wavelet *verification* in meteorology (Buschow & Friederichs) compares local wavelet spectra: how variance is distributed over scales, anisotropy and direction. It does *not* compare cross-scale dependencies of envelopes. Hilbert-envelope methods are established in synoptic meteorology (Zimin et al. 2003, 2006; Wolf & Wirth 2015) for Rossby-wave-packet tracking, including on *forecast-difference* fields. They are used within one band, not to correlate envelopes across bands. Operational nowcasting (S-PROG/STEPS/pysteps) uses exactly the same building block — Gaussian band-pass octave cascades — but models each level separately.

### Cited Findings
- Buschow & Friederichs (2020), "Using wavelets to verify the scale structure of precipitation forecasts", Adv. Stat. Climatol. Meteorol. Oceanogr. 6:13-30, doi:10.5194/ascmo-6-13-2020 [V; page range U]. Wavelet structure scores built on local wavelet spectra answer "whether the predicted rainfall variability is distributed correctly across a range of spatial scales" — [ASCMO](https://doi.org/10.5194/ascmo-6-13-2020)
- Buschow & Friederichs (2021), "SAD: Verifying the scale, anisotropy and direction of precipitation forecasts", QJRMS, doi:10.1002/qj.3964 [V; volume/pages U]. Scale, orientation and degree of anisotropy are evaluated separately using a dual-tree complex wavelet transform. An inverse-wavelet post-processing corrects structure errors — [QJRMS](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3964); R package sad/dualtrees — [CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/sad/sad.pdf)
- Buschow, Pidstrigach & Friederichs (2019) used a 2D redundant discrete wavelet transform (building on Kapp et al. 2018) to analyse the scale of variability globally and per grid point [described in Buschow & Friederichs 2021; primary U] — [SAD paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3964)
- Buschow's thesis, "Spatial Verification with Wavelets" (Univ. Bonn, 2022): grid-point scores "tend to prefer smoother, less detailed predictions and fail to appraise the realism of the simulated spatial structure" — [hdl 20.500.11811/9723](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.11811/9723)
- Zimin, Szunyogh, Patil, Hunt & Ott (2003), "Extracting envelopes of Rossby wave packets", MWR 131:1011-1017, doi:10.1175/1520-0493(2003)131<1011:EEORWP>2.0.CO;2 [V]. Band-limited (k 4-9) Hilbert envelope of 300 hPa v. Used to track analysis-error and targeted-observation impact in NCEP analyses. The authors say Hilbert envelopes had not previously been used for atmospheric wave packets — [MWR](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/131/5/1520-0493_2003_131_1011_eeorwp_2.0.co_2.xml)
- Zimin, Szunyogh, Hunt & Ott (2006), MWR 134:1329-1333, doi:10.1175/MWR3122.1 [V]. Envelope along streamlines. The envelope is extracted from the *difference between two forecasts* (k 4-11). ECMWF used the 2003 technique for error tracking (per personal communication cited there) — [MWR 2006](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/134/4/mwr3122.1.xml)
- Wolf & Wirth (2015), MWR, doi:10.1175/MWR-D-14-00120.1 [V]. The Hilbert envelope spuriously fragments wave packets because of semigeostrophic higher harmonics. Cure: smoothing or semigeostrophic coordinates — [Wolf & Wirth](https://doi.org/10.1175/mwr-d-14-00120.1)
- Seed (2003), "A dynamic and spatial scaling approach to advection forecasting", J. Appl. Meteorol. 42(3):381-388 [V-partial]. S-PROG decomposes radar rain fields into a cascade of octave levels (e.g. 128-256, 64-128 km...) with spectral filters. Each level is evolved by its own AR(2) model with level-specific Lagrangian autocorrelations — [conference version](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/20971.pdf)
- Bowler, Pierce & Seed (2006), "STEPS...", QJRMS 132, doi:10.1256/qj.04.100 [V] — [QJRMS](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.04.100). pysteps implements "Gaussian bandpass filters in logarithmic frequency scale" for the cascade decomposition — [pysteps docs](https://pysteps.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pysteps%5Freference/cascade.html); [sprog.py](https://github.com/pySTEPS/pysteps/blob/master/pysteps/nowcasts/sprog.py)

### Inferences
- In meteorological verification, the scale-dependence of *variance* (spectra, wavelet spectra, SAD) is well covered. The *coupling of local amplitudes between adjacent scales* is not, as far as these searches show. Two fields can have identical wavelet spectra (identical Buschow scale scores) but different adjacent-band envelope coupling. This is exactly the information that phase-randomised surrogates destroy and that the anchored metric retains.
- The STEPS/S-PROG octave Gaussian band-pass cascade is a citable precedent for the *decomposition* used by the inheritance metric. STEPS treats the levels separately (per-level AR models). This gives a concrete applied context in which cross-level envelope coupling is ignored, and a natural test-bed for the metric.
- Zimin et al. (2006) show that band-limited envelopes of *forecast-difference* fields are an accepted tool in NWP error analysis. This supports the methodological legitimacy of envelope diagnostics on forecast fields.

### Gaps
- Wavelet bicoherence / bispectral cross-scale diagnostics in atmospheric model evaluation: not searched in depth. No result surfaced.
- Kapp et al. (2018) and Buschow et al. (2019) primaries not retrieved.

## Q5. Scale-interaction / modulation diagnostics in atmospheric dynamics (Nastrom-Gage, effective resolution, mesoscale modulation): any explicit band-envelope correlation?

### Takeaway
I found no atmospheric paper that computes a correlation between local envelopes (amplitudes) of *adjacent spatial scale bands* as a diagnostic, either in reanalysis or as a model-evaluation metric. The closest atmospheric cross-scale *coupling* work is temporal, not spatial: Paluš (2014) on phase-amplitude coupling across time scales in station temperature, tested with Fourier and multifractal surrogates. Spectral evaluation (energy spectra, effective resolution) dominates the model-evaluation literature, including for ML models.

### Cited Findings
- Paluš (2014), "Multiscale atmospheric dynamics: cross-frequency phase-amplitude coupling in the air temperature", PRL 112:078702, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.078702 [V]. Conditional mutual information shows that the phase of 6-11 yr oscillations influences the amplitude of variability at scales from months to 4-5 yr in about a century of European daily SAT. It is "statistically tested using the Fourier-transform and multifractal surrogate data methods" — [PRL](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.112.078702)
- Paluš (2014), "Cross-scale interactions and information transfer", Entropy 16(10):5263, doi:10.3390/e16105263 [V]. Lists phase-phase, amplitude-amplitude and phase-amplitude cross-scale relations. Found no phase-phase interaction in SAT, only directed phase→amplitude — [Entropy](https://doi.org/10.3390/e16105263)
- A targeted search for correlations between the local amplitude of mesoscale and synoptic band-passed fields returned only case studies, PV diagnostics, gravity-wave high-pass methods and local wave activity — no envelope-correlation diagnostic — [search results incl. Hirt et al. 2023 QJRMS on meso-synoptic interactions](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/108866/1/Quart_J_Royal_Meteoro_Soc_-_2023_-_Hirt_-_Scale_interactions_between_the_meso%E2%80%90_and_synoptic_scales_and_the_impact_of.pdf)
- Bonavita (2024), GRL, doi:10.1029/2023GL107377 [V]. ML model (Pangu, FourCastNet, GraphCast) spectra diverge from ERA5 from wavenumbers 60-80 (~500-700 km). Effective resolution is ~500-700 km and degrades with lead time; ML forecasts are "overly smooth"; physical balances are unrealistic. ML spectra differ from the ECMWF *ensemble-mean* spectra: the ML models lack the synoptic-scale energy drop of the EM at 3-5 days but show reduced small-scale variability — [GRL](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023gl107377)
- Selz & Craig (2023), GRL 50:e2023GL105747 [V-partial: cited in Bonavita 2024 and ECMWF slides]. Pangu-Weather fails to reproduce realistic error growth from small perturbations — [Bonavita 2024](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023GL107377)
- [U] Standard references not re-verified here: Nastrom & Gage (1985), JAS 42:950-960 (canonical spectrum); Skamarock (2004), MWR 132:3019-3032, doi:10.1175/MWR2830.1 (effective resolution from KE spectra). Both are spectral, not envelope-coupling, diagnostics.

### Inferences
- Bonavita (2024) is the natural foil for the inheritance metric. It diagnoses ML smoothing via *spectra*. The anchored inheritance metric is by construction blind to the spectrum, because surrogates share it, and measures something orthogonal. The Phase-33 finding — that the IFS ensemble mean also over-inherits, like ML systems — parallels Bonavita's EM-vs-ML spectral comparison. It extends that comparison to a non-spectral (phase-dependent) property. This is a defensible novelty angle.
- Paluš's work is the closest atmospheric precedent for *surrogate-tested cross-scale amplitude coupling*. It is temporal, phase→amplitude, and not used for model evaluation.

### Gaps
- Menchaca & Durran papers on mesoscale spectra/mountain waves were not retrieved or verified. I cannot confirm whether any of them contains an envelope-modulation diagnostic (I believe not; unverified).
- Not searched: "synoptic modulation of mesoscale variance" in the convection-permitting-model literature (e.g. variance of precipitation conditioned on synoptic regime). This is conditional-variance work, related but not envelope-envelope correlation.

## Q6. LES / subgrid modelling: do tests check intermittency, backscatter or scale coupling?

### Takeaway
LES a-posteriori testing historically focused on first- and second-order statistics. A smaller literature tests higher-order moments, intermittency (structure-function exponents, flatness) and correlations between resolved-scale increments and SGS quantities. Some of these are cross-scale in spirit (Linkmann, Buzzicotti & Biferale 2018). None uses an envelope-envelope band correlation.

### Cited Findings
- Meneveau & Katz (2000), "Scale-invariance and turbulence models for large-eddy simulation", Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 32:1-32, doi:10.1146/annurev.fluid.32.1.1 [V]. Reviews a-priori and a-posteriori criteria and the effects of intermittency, coherent structures and non-equilibrium on SGS modelling — [Annual Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.fluid.32.1.1)
- Bou-Zeid (2015), "Challenging the large eddy simulation technique with advanced a posteriori tests", JFM Focus, doi:10.1017/jfm.2014.616 [V; volume U]. A-posteriori tests "have historically focused on the first- and second-order statistics". LES can match those while failing higher orders (citing Pan, Chamecki & Isard 2014). Stevens, Wilczek & Meneveau (2014, JFM 757:888-907) need more than 90% resolved variance to reproduce log-laws of even moments up to order 10 — [Bou-Zeid 2015](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2014.616)
- Biferale, Bonaccorso, Buzzicotti & Iyer (2019), "Self-similar subgrid-scale models for inertial range turbulence and accurate measurements of intermittency", PRL 123:014503, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.014503 [V]. Tests SGS closures by their reproduction of anomalous exponents. Finds that "intermittency for high-order moments is not captured by many of the popular phenomenological models" — [PRL](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.014503)
- Linkmann, Buzzicotti & Biferale (2018), "Multi-scale properties of large eddy simulations: correlations between resolved-scale velocity-field increments and subgrid-scale quantities", J. Turbul. 19(6):493-527, doi:10.1080/14685248.2018.1462497 [V-partial: from reference lists]. Per Vela-Martín (2022), the Smagorinsky model reproduces the multifractal scaling of structure functions well (Linkmann et al. 2018) — [Vela-Martín 2022, JFM](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.123); [ref list in JFM "Scale invariance of intermittency in LES turbulence"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/scale-invariance-of-intermittency-in-les-turbulence/C105AFABEF9328A6276C0D072C268BB0)
- Vela-Martín (2022), "Subgrid-scale models of isotropic turbulence need not produce energy backscatter", JFM 937:A14 [V-partial: doi:10.1017/jfm.2022.123 verified, article number U]. Backscatter represents conservative spatial fluxes. Intermittency of SGS fluxes can be removed but "should be modelled as a robust feature of the SGS interactions" — [JFM](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.123)
- "Scale invariance of intermittency in LES turbulence" (JFM; arXiv:2504.15874; authors per refs Mailybaev and co-workers, U). Smagorinsky-type closures respect hidden scale invariance at resolved and subgrid scales — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15874v3)
- Burton & Dahm (2005), Phys. Fluids 17:075112: multifractal SGS model with backscatter limiter, a-posteriori tests [V-partial] — [Phys. Fluids](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article/17/7/075112/908325/Multifractal-subgrid-scale-modeling-for-large-eddy)

### Inferences
- In LES, the evaluation analogue of "inheritance fidelity" would be a cross-scale coupling test of whether resolved-scale amplitudes near the cutoff inherit modulation from larger resolved scales correctly. Linkmann et al. (2018) is the nearest precedent: correlations between resolved increments and SGS quantities. It uses increments and SGS fluxes, not band envelopes.

### Gaps
- Pan, Chamecki & Isard (2014) not retrieved. Exact bibliographic details for Linkmann et al. (2018) come from reference lists only.

## Q7. ML turbulence/weather surrogates: are cross-scale or intermittency metrics used to show that MSE models smooth wrongly while generative ones preserve statistics?

### Takeaway
In ML *turbulence*, intermittency metrics (flatness, structure-function exponents, PDF tails of increments) are standard benchmarks for generative models (Li et al. 2024, NMI). Some authors state explicitly that most NN turbulence works evaluate only second-order statistics. In ML *weather*, published "physical consistency" critiques rely mostly on spectra and balance diagnostics (Bonavita 2024). I found no published use of cross-scale *envelope coupling* or cascade statistics to evaluate ML weather models. The wavelet-scattering-spectra framework (Mallat group) contains cross-scale wavelet-modulus correlations by construction and is used to validate generative models of physical fields. It is the closest *form* match in the ML literature.

### Cited Findings
- Li, Biferale, Bonaccorso, Scarpolini & Buzzicotti (2024), "Synthetic Lagrangian turbulence by generative diffusion models", Nature Machine Intelligence 6:393-403, doi:10.1038/s42256-024-00810-0 [V-partial; volume/pages U]. A diffusion model reproduces fat-tailed increment PDFs, anomalous scaling and increased intermittency near the dissipative scale. Slight deviations below the dissipative scale appear in acceleration and flatness — [NMI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00810-0)
- "A multiscale and multicriteria Generative Adversarial Network to synthesize 1-dimensional turbulent fields" (arXiv:2307.16580; authors U). "very few of these works incorporate higher-order statistics in the training procedure, and the most only evaluate ... second-order statistics ... mainly the power spectrum". Such works "do not study the capacity of their models to reproduce the energy cascade ... or the intermittency phenomenon" — [arXiv 2307.16580](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16580)
- Morel, Rochette, Leonarduzzi, Bouchaud & Mallat (2022), "Scale dependencies and self-similar models with wavelet scattering spectra", arXiv:2204.10177 [V]. "Dependencies across scales are captured by the joint correlation across time and scales of wavelet coefficients and their modulus". Applied to turbulence and financial series; maximum-entropy generative models are conditioned on these statistics — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10177)
- Cheng, Morel, Allys, Ménard & Mallat (2024), "Scattering spectra models for physics", PNAS Nexus 3:pgae103, doi:10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae103 [V-partial; volume/article from DOI, year 2024 per journal, arXiv 2023]. Covariances of scattering coefficients (wavelet transform + modulus) model multiscale physical fields and reproduce moments up to 4th order. Uses include classification, inference and validation — [PNAS Nexus](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae103)
- Portilla & Simoncelli (2000), "A parametric texture model based on joint statistics of complex wavelet coefficients", Int. J. Comput. Vis. 40(1):49-71, doi:10.1023/A:1026553619983 [V]. Includes the "cross correlation of subband magnitudes with all orientations at a coarser scale" (coarse band upsampled). Synthesis fails without the magnitude-correlation constraints. Simoncelli & Portilla (1998, ICIP) "Texture characterization via joint statistics of wavelet coefficient magnitudes" — [IJCV reprint](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/eero/portilla99-reprint.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1026553619983)
- Turbulence generative/diffusion papers evaluating structure functions, flatness and dissipation moments: "Turbulence scaling from deep learning diffusion generative models" (arXiv:2311.06112); "Spectrally decomposed diffusion models for generative turbulence recovery" (arXiv:2312.15029); "Stochastic multiscale reconstruction of Lagrangian turbulence via guided diffusion" (arXiv:2606.05783, wavelet coarse-graining, recovers flatness and local exponents) [all arXiv-level, authors U] — [2311.06112](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06112v2); [2312.15029](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15029v1); [2606.05783](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05783)
- Bonavita (2024): ML weather blurriness is expected because L2/L1 training converges to the conditional mean (Bishop 1995) — [GRL](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023gl107377); [ECMWF slides](https://dataserver.cptec.inpe.br/dataserver_dimnt/monan/meeting_AI_2024_06_19a21/MONAN%20DAY%202/MONAN_AI_2024-AI_models_evaluation-Massimo.pdf)
- Li, Carver, Lopez-Gomez, Sha & Anderson (2024), "Generative emulation of weather forecast ensembles with diffusion models" (SEEDS), Science Advances, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adk4489 [V-partial]. Evaluated with ensemble/probabilistic metrics, not cascade statistics (per abstract-level reading) — [Sci. Adv.](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk4489)

### Inferences
- The "deterministic smooths, generative preserves" narrative is well established in ML turbulence via intermittency metrics, and in ML weather via spectra. An *envelope-coupling* diagnostic applied to ML weather models and to an NWP ensemble mean appears to be unoccupied territory, subject to Gaps below. The Phase-33 Arm-C result (IFS ENS mean over-inherits too: an averaging effect, not a neural-net effect) is consistent with Bonavita's conditional-mean argument. It is novel in showing the effect in a spectrum-anchored coupling statistic.
- The scattering-spectra literature is the strongest *form* competitor. A reviewer from ML/physics will likely ask how the inheritance metric differs from a (restricted) scattering cross-spectrum S(j1, j2) between |W_j1 x| and |W_j2 x| at adjacent j. Answer: it is close to a single entry of that matrix, with log + rank + surrogate anchoring + time median. The author should cite it and say so.

## Q8. Synthesis — closest prior art and how the inheritance metric differs

### Takeaway
No single prior work combines all of: (i) adjacent-octave band-pass of a geophysical field; (ii) log-envelopes smoothed at the coarse edge; (iii) *spatial* rank correlation between adjacent-band envelopes; (iv) anchoring by subtracting a Fourier-phase-surrogate baseline; (v) use as a forecast-vs-reanalysis evaluation diagnostic across NWP, ensemble-mean and ML systems. Each ingredient has precedent. A novelty claim should be framed at the level of *the combination and its use* (spectrum-anchored envelope coupling as a forecast-fidelity diagnostic), not at the level of "cross-scale envelope correlation" as a concept.

### Cited Findings
Closest prior art, ranked by closeness of form (F) and use (U):
1. **Portilla & Simoncelli (2000)**, cross-scale magnitude correlations of complex wavelet coefficients at adjacent scales. F: very close (adjacent-scale magnitude correlation over space). U: texture model and synthesis-validation statistic, not geophysical — [IJCV](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1026553619983)
2. **Wavelet scattering spectra (Morel et al. 2022; Cheng et al. 2024)**, cross-scale correlations of wavelet moduli. F: close (a superset). U: models and validation of physical fields, including turbulence — [arXiv 2204.10177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10177); [PNAS Nexus](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae103)
3. **Arneodo et al. (1998)**, space-scale correlation functions of log wavelet amplitudes. F: close (log-amplitude cross-scale correlation) with an analytical cascade prediction. U: cascade identification in turbulence, not model evaluation — [PRL](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.80.708)
4. **Bruns et al. (2000)**, amplitude envelope correlation across frequencies. F: close in time domain. U: neuroscience coupling detection — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10841367/)
5. **Mathis et al. (2009)** AM coefficient and refinements. F: related (signal vs envelope, not envelope vs envelope). U: physics of wall turbulence. LES studies quantify it but do not use it as a formal acceptance metric — [JFM](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022112009006946)
6. **Stolle et al. (2009, 2012); Lovejoy & Schertzer (2010)**. F: different (moment-scaling of a flux proxy). U: *very close* — cascade statistics of reanalyses and operational forecasts compared across models and lead times — [NPG](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-16-607-2009); [QJRMS](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1916)
7. **Paluš (2014)**. F: cross-scale (phase→amplitude) coupling in atmospheric data with Fourier/multifractal surrogates. U: discovery, not evaluation — [PRL](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.112.078702)
8. **Buschow & Friederichs (2020, 2021)**. F: wavelet scale spectra (no cross-scale coupling). U: forecast verification — [ASCMO](https://doi.org/10.5194/ascmo-6-13-2020)
9. **S-PROG/STEPS (Seed 2003; Bowler et al. 2006)**. F: identical decomposition (octave Gaussian band-pass cascade), levels evolved separately. U: nowcasting — [QJRMS](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.04.100)

Methodological cautions from the prior art that apply directly:
- Envelope-type cross-scale correlations are tied to higher moments: skewness for R (Schlatter & Örlü 2010; Duvvuri & McKeon 2015); intermittency for log-amplitude correlations (Arneodo et al. 1998). The audit's rho +0.69 with log-envelope variance should be reported as expected, not as a nuisance — [Schlatter & Örlü](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/22/5/051704/257529/Quantifying-the-interaction-between-large-and); [Duvvuri & McKeon](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2015.79)
- Filter choice materially changes envelope-correlation values (Doğan et al. 2018) — [FDR](https://doi.org/10.1088/1873-7005/aaca81)
- Neighbouring-band anticorrelation can arise from local scale (frequency) modulation alone (Hyafil 2015) — [Front. Neurosci.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00370)
- An envelope correlation is not proof of dynamical scale interaction (Cui & Jacobi 2025) — [JFM](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2024.1203)
- Surrogate choice matters (Aru et al.; Frontiers MEG 2016). Fourier-phase surrogates preserve the spectrum but also remove any non-Gaussian single-band structure. The anchored quantity therefore mixes "coupling" with "non-Gaussianity of each band" — [Front. Comput. Neurosci. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2016.00120/full)

### Inferences
- Defensible novelty: (a) a spectrum-anchored (phase-surrogate) adjacent-band log-envelope rank correlation for 2D geophysical fields; (b) applied as a forecast-fidelity diagnostic showing that conditional-mean products (ML deterministic forecasts *and* NWP ensemble means) over-inherit relative to ERA5, while single physics-based members do not. To my search depth, (b) has no precedent in either the turbulence-ML or weather-ML literature.
- Not defensible as novel: "using cross-scale statistics to evaluate NWP/reanalysis" (Stolle/Lovejoy did it, including lead-time dependence); "envelope correlation between frequency bands" (Bruns 2000; Portilla-Simoncelli 2000); "surrogate-tested cross-scale coupling in atmospheric data" (Paluš 2014).
- Suggested additional controls derived from the prior art: (1) relate the metric explicitly to the adjacent-scale entry of a scattering cross-spectrum, or to the Portilla-Simoncelli magnitude correlation, and show added value; (2) test a synthetic wavenumber-drifting single-band field for Hyafil-type spurious anticorrelation; (3) test a multiplicative-cascade synthetic field (Arneodo-type W-cascade) as a positive control with known log-amplitude cross-scale correlation; (4) report sensitivity to filter shape, following Doğan et al.

### Gaps
- Not done in this session: full forward-citation search on Mathis 2009, Bruns 2000, Portilla-Simoncelli 2000 and Morel 2022 for any use on atmospheric model output. This is the most likely place an unseen precedent could exist, e.g. scattering-transform evaluation of climate-model or ML-emulator fields.
- No source located for a quantitative relation between Fourier-phase-surrogate-anchored envelope correlation and cascade intermittency parameters. That link is inferred, not cited.
