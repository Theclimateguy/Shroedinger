## Description (plain text)

Two compact descriptors of the cross-scale organization of the atmospheric circulation are introduced and tested on reanalysis data: the level-coupling profile P — the spatial rank correlation between the activity maps of adjacent octave levels of the 850 hPa relative vorticity field — and the inter-level transfer asymmetry index A — the norm of the commutator of the estimated aggregation and refinement operators, that is, a measure of how irreversible the transition between levels of description is.

Material: ERA5 for 12 regions of 20°×40° representing six circulation regimes, January–March and July–September seasons of 2017–2024 (96 region-windows), with independent replication on MERRA-2. The design rests on preregistered criteria, surrogate nulls, and confirmation of every claim on samples that took no part in obtaining it.

Both descriptors form stable regional signatures (p = 0.001) that persist across seasons, years and ENSO phases. The index A carries information beyond the power spectrum and beyond the profile P, is robust to controls for domain geometry and for the effective resolution of the reanalysis, and its regional geography is reproduced across reanalyses (Spearman ρ = 0.93, p < 1e-4). The largest values occur over moist-convective oceanic regimes and the smallest over arid continental ones; the Congo and Amazon basins, under a similar regime, exhibit qualitatively different hierarchical architectures. A catalogue of tested and rejected interpretations delimits the range of valid application: the descriptors do not measure the cross-scale flux of kinetic energy, obey no simple law in environmental covariates, are level-specific, and carry no information about the moisture-budget residual of a reanalysis.

The work continues the line of research into the hierarchical organization of geosystems associated with Yu. G. Puzachenko — from the identification of levels to the measurement of their interaction — and proposes applications to objective regionalization and to the a priori assessment of statistical downscaling.
